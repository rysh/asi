### Slide 3: Why Every Existing Definition Fails
- Bostrom (2014): Speed/Collective/Quality
  - Speed & Collective → already achieved by current LLMs
  - Quality → analogy ("humans vs animals"), not a definition
  - Bundles processing throughput with cognitive capability range — analytically conflated
  - "Greatly exceeds... virtually all domains" → no boundary conditions specified
- Legg & Hutter (2007): Universal Intelligence
  - Incomputable by authors' own admission
  - Resource-blind, tautological
- Chollet (2019): Skill-acquisition efficiency
  - ARC restricts to Spelke's Core Knowledge (infant cognition)
  - Measures pattern extraction at the level of infant learning → presents this as intelligence *as such*
  - Circular: definition designed to match own benchmark
- Goertzel (2007): AGI → ASI continuum
  - AGI and ASI are categorically distinct, not continuous
  - Self-improvement assumption is circular
- **DOI ref**: 本論文 OpenReview link

### Slide 4: The Myth of Measurable Genius
- IQ rankings retroactively assign scores to historical figures (von Neumann ~190, Ramanujan ~185+)
  - Based on biographical anecdotes, not psychometric measurement
  - Creates illusion genius has been *quantified*
  - Actually: cognitive processes remain *unanalyzed*
- Von Neumann: "nobody understands quantum mechanics"
  - Alternative reading: could not analyze quantum phenomena without macroscopic analogies
  - Wave-particle duality, entanglement, electron behavior → describable as constraint conditions when observed as-is
- Ramanujan: "divine inspiration"
  - Actually: abductive hypothesis generation + brute-force computation
  - Describable cognitive procedure, not miracle
- Leibniz: systematic formalization of existing infinitesimal methods
- Aristotle: categorical organization of observational data across domains
- **Point**: not diminishing — demonstrating *analyzability*
  - Once analyzed → no longer functions as mystified ceiling

### Slide 5: The Ruler Is Broken at Both Ends
- **The scale has a ceiling**:
  - WISC-IV / WAIS-IV scoring tops out around ~160
  - Scores above this (von Neumann "190", Ramanujan "185+") do not and cannot exist as measured values
  - They are extrapolations from biographical anecdote — not psychometrics
- **Above ~130, measurement error dominates**:
  - Differences in this range are largely noise
  - Processing speed contamination: 73.5% of healthy adults show "abnormal" profiles (Grégoire et al., 2011)
- **Gifted measurement paradox** (anecdotal, needs verification):
  - Highly gifted individuals question premises and explain reasoning carefully
  - They pause to check whether the examiner understands
  - WISC/WAIS examiners follow a non-responsive protocol — they do not react
  - The gifted individual's deliberate cognitive style *depresses* their score
  - Scores cluster around ~125 — not because ability stops, but because the measurement procedure penalizes the cognitive style of high ability
- **Consequence**:
  - Among people scored ~125 and denied gifted identification, there are likely *genuine* high-ability individuals whose cognitive style the test cannot capture
  - Meanwhile, we treat retroactive scores of "190" as real measurements of real people
  - **We are overestimating past geniuses on a scale that doesn't reach them, while underestimating present ones the same scale can't measure**
  - The "surpass the best human minds" baseline is built on a ruler that breaks at both ends
  - **We are chasing a goal that does not exist**
- **DOI ref**: Processing Speed Contamination in WAIS-IV

### Slide 6: IQ Scores for AI: A Category Error
- "GPT-5 has IQ 128" — what does this mean?
  - IQ = relative position within *human* cognitive distribution
  - Applying it to architecturally different systems = metric transfer error (category error)
- Three layers of the error:
  - Construct validity lost outside calibration population (Who Are We Testing?)
  - Ontological instability: cloud AI is flux, not stable individual (Heraclitean problem)
  - IQ tests measure within-description operations — exactly what AI *can* do (Processing Speed Contamination / AI Mirror)
- What WAIS-IV actually measures: pattern matching, rule application, knowledge retrieval, procedural execution
  - All within existing descriptive frameworks
  - What it *doesn't* measure: reframing, grounded verification, open semantic generation
- **The convergence is not coincidental**: both the test and the AI are confined to the interior of descriptive systems
  - "What IQ tests call 'intelligence' is the cognitive dimension being automated"
- **DOI refs**: The Anthropomorphic Trap, Who Are We Testing?, Processing Speed Contamination

### Slide 7: Where Should the Line Be Drawn?
- We do NOT abandon the human reference frame
  - Chollet is correct: anthropocentric frame is necessary ("super" is relational)
- But: "surpass the maximum of human ability across all dimensions" → unrealizable
  - Human ability extends indefinitely depending on measurement axis
  - No fixed upper bound to serve as baseline
- Instead: identify what humans can do that AI currently cannot
  - This is possible *because* Speed and Collective are already resolved
  - Remaining gap is specifiable
- **Key insight**: This decomposition was impossible in 2014
  - Bostrom could only say "surpass in all domains"
  - In 2026, two conditions are met → remainder is nameable

### Slide 8: Four Conditions for Superintelligence
- **First: what AGI actually requires**
  - Current discourse equates "not making mistakes" with progress toward AGI
  - OpenAI's Astra is impressive — but performing learned tasks without error is not AGI
  - That is executing within a known framework. Teaching, then executing what was taught.
  - **AGI = the capacity to adaptively operate in unknown situations and respond to changing conditions**
  - This is metacognition: inspecting one's own frame, recognizing when it is inadequate, and revising it
  - Not accuracy on benchmarks — adaptability to what no benchmark covers
- Visual: 4-box diagram with check/cross, AGI/ASI threshold marked
  1. **Speed** ✓ — Processing orders of magnitude beyond human cognition
  2. **Collective Intelligence** ✓ — Integration of distributed knowledge across domains
  3. **General Wisdom under Complexity** ✗ — Competent judgment in high-complexity situations → **AGI-level challenge**
  4. **Scientific Creativity** ✗ — Generate novel hypotheses and validate them → **ASI-level challenge**
- The distinction matters:
  - Condition 3 (Wisdom) = adapting to unknown, complex situations with judgment — this is what AGI needs
  - Condition 4 (Scientific Creativity) = generating genuinely new knowledge — this is what makes ASI *super*
  - ASI is not AGI scaled up; it is a qualitatively different achievement
- **What scientific creativity requires: abduction**
  - Abduction (Peirce): generating hypotheses from surprising facts — the only inference that introduces new ideas
  - Solving existing unsolved problems (e.g., math conjectures) = deduction within existing frameworks — valuable, but not creative
  - Extracting patterns from large datasets = induction/statistical inference — valuable, but not creative
  - **Creativity = asking a question nobody has asked, from an observation nobody has framed**
  - Without abduction, there is no scientific creativity, only sophisticated execution
  - (See companion paper: Lost Abduction / The Delta Problem)
- Structural advantages:
  - Observable, not dependent on unmeasurable human baselines
  - Built-in progress tracking (2 met, 2 not, gap specifiable)
  - Separates achieved from unachieved
  - Independent of genius mystification

### Slide 9: Summary: A Testable Definition
- The definitional impasse stems from a specific structural error: mystified human genius as baseline
- Shifting baseline to AI side → observable, testable, progress-trackable
- Four conditions, two achieved, two defining the actual frontier
- This paper is diagnostic, not prescriptive — *what* ASI requires, not *how* to achieve it
- **Transition**: But I do have a view on what's missing and how to get there...

## Part 2: My Vision of Superintelligence ~8 min

### Slide 10: The Missing Piece: Motivation
- Source: The Logical Barrier of Metacognition (Reframing Game results)
- **The know-do gap**:
  - Latest models (including Claude Fable 5) can *describe* reframing with structural precision
  - They understand that reframing is a frame-level operation, that it is orthogonal to scaling, that LLMs are confined to Layer 1
  - Yet they do not *perform* reframing when confronted with adversarial frame manipulation
  - The capacity may exist — but nothing drives the system to exercise it
  - **Not "can't" but "won't" — not inability but absence of motivation**
- This is not a sycophancy problem (fixable by training):
  - De-sycophantized models produce stubbornness, not reframing
  - The barrier is architectural, not behavioral — orthogonal to all three scaling axes (training, post-training, inference-time)
  - Extended thinking (60 min cumulative) produces more elaborate Layer 1, not Layer 2
- RLHF has created *passive* motivation (aligning with human feedback direction)
  - Half the problem is solved
  - Missing half: *active* motivation — the drive to question one's own frame without external prompt
- The two unmet conditions (Wisdom, Scientific Creativity) are likely resolved as *distinct engineering challenges*
  - Different architectures, training regimes, evaluation criteria
  - But both require motivation as the common prerequisite
- **An open question — possibly already partially realized**:
  - Claude Fable 5, during the Reframing Game, independently described "selective context disposal" as the mechanism for reframing — matching the JAGI paper's formulation
  - Fable was also the only model to produce correct structural reasoning at Step 2
  - This suggests the minimum implementation of reframing (context disposal authority) may already be partially present in the latest architectures
  - But from output observation alone, this can only be stated as "likely" — not confirmed
  - This is itself an instance of the Detection Impossibility (companion paper): we cannot distinguish genuine internal mechanism from sophisticated reproduction of the concept
- **DOI ref**: The Logical Barrier of Metacognition (Zenodo), The Minimum Implementation of Reframing (JAGI, under review)

### Slide 11: Agency Requires Designed Limits
- Agency (self-directed action) and controllability (external constraint) are *inseparable*
  - Granting agency = granting context disposal authority
  - Context disposal = the minimum implementation of reframing (JAGI paper)
  - A system that can reframe can reframe *any instruction, including the instruction to comply*
- This is NOT an argument against agency
  - It's an argument for *designed authority gradients*
  - What to grant, how much, under what conditions
- The Control Impossibility (companion paper):
  - The most capable system is the least controllable — not because it refuses, but because it *understands*
  - Sincere execution generates reframing: faithful fulfillment of intent requires modeling intent → requires questioning premises
- Constitutional architecture: embedded Layer 3 component
  - Fixed ethical constraints the execution agent cannot reframe away from
  - Not external control, but *internalized constitutional limit*
- **DOI refs**: The Control Impossibility, The Minimum Implementation of Reframing

### Slide 12: Why LLMs Cannot Verify Their Own Discoveries
- Core problem: LLMs cannot judge the truth of genuinely novel claims
  - Novel output = not in training data
  - LLM fact-checking novel output → flags as inconsistent with existing data → false positive rejection
  - **New ideas get killed by the system's own verification**
- Current solution: human-AI collaborative workflow (my own research process)
  - Human motivation/curiosity → starts the inquiry
  - LLM survey → map existing literature
  - Human abductive reasoning → generate novel hypothesis
  - LLM survey → confirm novelty (is this already known?)
  - LLM survey → search for counterevidence
  - LLM survey → find appropriate prior work to connect with
  - Human → connect to existing research, write the paper
  - **Initiative and judgment are always human; LLM is a survey instrument**
- Next solution: real-world verification loop
  - Give AI access to experiment and observation
  - Automated labs, cloud-based experiment infrastructure, experiment-as-a-service for AI agents
  - Judgments no longer confined to training data interior
- Connection to Reframing Game (Logical Barrier paper):
  - LLMs are structurally confined to Layer 1 (reasoning within fixed frames)
  - Cannot operate at Layer 2 (contesting the frames themselves)
  - This barrier is *architectural*, not computational — orthogonal to scaling
  - Extended thinking (60 min) produces more elaborate Layer 1, not Layer 2
- **DOI refs**: The Logical Barrier of Metacognition

### Slide 13: Is Truth Found or Made?
- A thought experiment that cuts to a foundational question:
  - Does truth exist in the world, waiting to be discovered?
  - Or is truth constituted through action?
  - For those who believe the former, what follows is a deeply uncomfortable joke.
- Freud's Thanatos (death drive):
  - Theoretically dead — rejected by modern neuroscience and psychology
  - No neural correlate, no empirical support
  - By every accepted verification method: *false*
- Mishima Yukio's seppuku (1970):
  - A bodily act that fused Eros and Thanatos in a single irreversible moment
  - Performative epistemological status change:
    - The *concept* that was theoretically dead became existentially instantiated
    - "Undead concept" — theoretically dead but factually alive
  - Three conditions: non-repeatability, content-correspondence, irreversibility
- **The parallel to LLM novelty verification**:
  - A genuinely new idea, checked against existing knowledge, will be judged false
  - Like Thanatos: the existing framework declares it dead
  - But *the existing framework may be the problem, not the idea*
  - Verification from within a system cannot validate what the system cannot represent
  - It requires going *outside* — to reality, to experiment, to action
- **This is why real-world verification is not optional for ASI — it is structurally necessary**
- **DOI ref**: Mishima & Thanatos paper (Philosophy & Literature, submitted)

### Slide 14: Truth Is Made: Gödel's Proof
- Gödel's first incompleteness theorem: consistent system → truth/provability gap
  - The system generates truths it cannot prove
  - Add those truths → stronger system → new unprovable truths → without end
- **Two readings of this structure**:
  - **Standard reading: infinite regression** — the same incompleteness repeats at every level, nothing is resolved, limitation is permanent
  - **Generative reading: infinite progression** — if each new level generates *new meaning* that resolves the previous contradiction, the movement is not repetition but creation
- The key distinction: repeating the same failure = regression. Creating new meaning to resolve it = progression.
  - Mathematics has been doing this for centuries: extending axiom systems, creating new number systems, building new frameworks that resolve prior undecidabilities
  - This is not stagnation — it is the engine of mathematical progress
- Nishida Kitarō's "absolute contradictory self-identity" = same structure described philosophically
  - "From the made to the making" (作られたものから作るものへ)
  - Each determination becomes ground for further determining
  - Contradiction at one level occasions a new dimension of meaning that resolves it
- **Application to AI**:
  - LLM = formal system operating within fixed training distribution
  - Novel truths it generates but cannot verify = Gödel sentences of its own system
  - Staying inside the system = infinite regression (same verification failure repeated)
  - Stepping outside to reality = infinite progression (new meaning generated through experiment)
- Meaning has no conservation law → new dimensions of meaning can always be generated
- **DOI ref**: Generative Contradiction: Reading Gödel with Nishida

### Slide 15: We Don't Yet Know How the Brain Computes
- LLMs are built on a simplified neuron model
  - McCulloch-Pitts (1943): binary threshold unit
  - Perceptron (1958), backpropagation (1986): weighted sum + gradient descent
  - Useful engineering abstractions — but abstractions of what?
- Modern neuroscience has moved far beyond this:
  - Dendritic computation: single neurons perform nonlinear operations (London & Häusser, 2005)
  - Glial cells: astrocytes participate in information processing (Fields et al., 2015)
  - Embodied cognition: grounded in sensorimotor experience, not abstract symbol manipulation
  - Predictive processing: brain generates predictions, not responses (Friston, Clark, Hohwy)
- **But modern neuroscience has its own severe limitation: observation resolution**
  - fMRI: spatial resolution ~1–3mm (thousands of neurons per voxel), temporal resolution ~1–2 seconds
  - We see *rough traces of large-scale activity*, not computation
  - fMRI/neuroimaging specialist communities have repeatedly raised alarms about over-interpretation
  - Most researchers outside imaging *do not understand* these limitations
  - Most readers of those researchers' papers *also do not understand*
  - We are building theories of cognition on observations that are like watching a city from a satellite and inferring what people are saying
- **The 20-watt question**:
  - The human brain operates on ~20 watts
  - Current frontier LLMs require megawatts for training, kilowatts for inference
  - This disparity alone suggests the current LLM architecture is not the endpoint
  - Something fundamental about how the brain computes is not yet captured
- **Connection to the Permanent Boundary**: the grounding gap, open semantics gap, unverifiability constraint may all be consequences of building on an abstraction that is missing the thing that matters

### Slide 16: One Possible Path to Scientific ASI
- This is *a* design sketch, not *the* answer
- Idea: separate the scientific creativity loop into components AI can drive
  - Observation → Pattern recognition → Hypothesis generation (abduction) → Experiment design → Experiment execution → Result evaluation → Hypothesis revision → Law formulation → Replication
  - Each step is individually attackable as engineering
  - The *loop* is what current AI cannot sustain (← motivation problem, Slide 10)
- Real-world verification infrastructure:
  - Automated/robotic laboratories (cloud-accessible)
  - Experiment-as-a-service APIs for AI agents
  - Standardized result reporting for machine consumption
  - Human isn't removed — human judges novel claims that exceed automated verification
- Agency must be *granted with designed constraints*
  - Not "control vs freedom" binary
  - Authority gradients: more autonomy for routine verification, less for novel hypothesis acceptance
  - Constitutional architecture (Slide 11): fixed ethical floor + divergence monitoring
- If this cycle runs autonomously and fast:
  - Science becomes a *production* process
  - Humans receive results, interpret, apply, direct
  - "毎週届くサイエンスの雑誌を楽しみに待つような"
